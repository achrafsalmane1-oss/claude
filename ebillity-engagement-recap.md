## Where Things Went Wrong

We want to be direct with you here, because you deserve a real explanation — not a sanitized one.

### 1. The Law Firms campaign destroyed our sending reputation early

The biggest campaign we ran — **eBillity – Law Firms** — had a **27.6% bounce rate**. Industry standard for cold email is under 2%. At 27.6%, we were hammering bad data at scale: 3,833 emails bounced out of 13,868 leads contacted.

This wasn't just a wasted campaign. High bounce rates are a signal to email providers (Google, Microsoft) that the sender isn't trustworthy. Once your sending domains and IPs get flagged, emails stop landing in inboxes regardless of how good your copy is. The damage compounds over time and across all campaigns running on the same infrastructure.

### 2. We hit 150 emails/day — the deliverability cliff

The most telling data point from the past week: **we're currently getting through roughly 150 emails/day** across the entire sending setup. When we started, infrastructure like this should be capable of 1,500–3,000+ per day per domain cluster. 150 emails/day means the inboxes are functionally burned — emails are either landing in spam or being deferred entirely.

This explains why later campaigns showed increasingly weak results even as the copy improved. The Timesheets campaign (0.61% reply rate), Hail Mary (0.97%), and the Loom/Call variants all had decent reply rates early but fell off as deliverability deteriorated. We were iterating on copy while fighting a deliverability fire underneath.

### 3. We over-indexed on law firms

Law firms were eBillity's natural ICP and where we started. But we hit them hard and repeatedly — the Law Firms campaign alone touched 13,868 contacts across 33,000+ emails. Smaller firms in particular have tight networks; one bad email experience circulates. We saturated the vertical before we had solid proof of concept and healthy infrastructure.

### 4. The ICP Variants campaign ran too large on a weakened foundation

The ICP Variants campaign became the biggest in the account at 50,360 emails sent. It ran while deliverability was already compromised. Despite 207 unique replies, only 2 marked interested — meaning most replies were objections or auto-responses. Running volume on a compromised infrastructure accelerated the problem rather than solving it.

## What We'd Do Differently

The core issue is infrastructure + vertical, not product-market fit. eBillity has a real product with real buyers outside of law. The engagement struggled because we were fighting a two-front war: burned deliverability + oversaturated vertical.

A reset with fresh infrastructure and untapped verticals is a legitimate path to results.
